Accessibility means making sure everyone can use your website, documents, messages and online services, including disabled people, older people and people with little confidence using technology.

## The law

The Equality Act 2010 applies to churches that provide services to the public. Churches must make reasonable adjustments so that disabled people aren't put at a substantial disadvantage. This includes information and services provided online.

The stricter website accessibility regulations for public bodies don't usually apply to churches. We recommend aiming for the same standard anyway, which is the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA.

## Your website

Check that your website:

- works on a phone, and when text is made larger
- can be used with a keyboard alone
- has text with enough contrast against its background
- gives images a short text description, unless they're decorative
- uses headings in a logical order
- has links that make sense on their own, not "click here"
- doesn't rely on colour alone to show meaning

If someone else builds your website, ask them to confirm it meets WCAG 2.2 level AA.

## Documents and messages

- write in plain English, with short sentences and clear headings
- use a clear font of at least 12 point in print
- don't put important information only in an image, such as a poster
- publish information as web pages where possible, as PDFs are often harder to use
- offer large print, audio or paper versions to people who ask

## Video, audio and livestreams

- turn on captions, and check automatic captions for mistakes
- describe important things that are only shown on screen
- give a text summary or transcript for important audio

## People who aren't online

Some people can't or don't want to use digital tools. Make sure they can still:

- hear about church news and events
- give money and sign up to rotas
- contact someone at the church

A printed notice sheet, a phone contact and a named person to help will make a big difference.

## Ask people what helps

The best way to find barriers is to ask. Invite people with different needs to try your website or a new system, and act on what they tell you.
