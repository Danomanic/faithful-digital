Good financial records protect your treasurer, reassure your givers and help your trustees make wise decisions. The right tool depends on the size of your church and how your accounts must be prepared.

## Know which type of accounts you need

Charity law sets how your accounts must be prepared, and this depends on your income, your legal structure and where you are in the UK.

Many smaller churches that aren't companies can prepare simpler "receipts and payments" accounts. Larger churches usually need "accruals" accounts, which follow the Charities Statement of Recommended Practice (SORP).

The financial thresholds change from time to time. Check the current rules with your regulator or denomination, or ask your independent examiner.

## A spreadsheet or accounting software?

| Feature | A spreadsheet | Accounting software |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Small churches with few transactions | Churches with more transactions, staff or funds to track |
| Cost | Usually free | A monthly fee, often with a charity discount |
| Bank feeds | Enter transactions yourself | Can import transactions from your bank automatically |
| Restricted funds | Possible with careful setup | Usually built in, or available as an add-on |
| Risk | Errors in formulas, and files held by one person | Less risk of errors, and easier to hand over |

A well-organised spreadsheet is a reasonable choice for many small churches. Keep it in a shared church folder, not on one person's computer.

## Things to keep in good order

Whatever tool you use:

- keep restricted funds, such as money given for a building project, separate from general funds
- reconcile your records with your bank statements every month
- keep receipts and invoices, either on paper or as scanned copies
- keep Gift Aid records linked to the donations they relate to
- make sure at least 2 people can approve payments, and no one approves their own expenses
- prepare a simple report for your trustees at each meeting

## Online banking

Most banks offer charity accounts with online banking. Look for an account that lets you:

- set up more than one user, with different permissions
- require 2 people to approve payments
- give view-only access to someone who checks the accounts

Use the security features your bank offers, and never share banking logins.

## Plan for handover

Treasurers change. Make it easier for the next one by:

- writing down how your accounts work, including any spreadsheet formulas
- keeping everything in church-owned accounts and folders
- agreeing a handover period when someone steps down
