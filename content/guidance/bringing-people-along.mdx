Most digital changes succeed or fail because of people, not technology. A change that's well explained and well supported will usually go smoothly. One that's rushed can leave people confused or left out.

## Help your trustees make a good decision

Give your trustees a short paper for any significant decision. Include:

- the problem you're trying to solve
- the options you've considered, including doing nothing
- what each option costs, now and in future years
- the risks, including data protection and what happens if you want to leave
- your recommendation, and why

Keep it to 1 or 2 pages. Give trustees time to read it before the meeting, and answer their questions honestly.

Download our [decision paper template](/templates/decision-paper/) to adapt for your church.

## Explain the change to your congregation

Tell people:

- what's changing, and when
- why you're making the change, and how it will help
- what they need to do, if anything
- where to get help

Use more than one channel, such as a notice in the service, your weekly news and a printed note. Repeat the message more than once.

## Help people who are less confident

Some people will find any change to technology difficult. Plan for this from the start.

- offer short help sessions, in person, at a time that suits people
- ask confident volunteers to sit alongside people who need help
- write simple step-by-step instructions, with screenshots
- keep a way for people to take part without the new system, if possible

Be patient and avoid jargon. Never make anyone feel foolish for asking for help.

## Listen and adjust

After a change, ask people how it's going. Ask what's working and what isn't, and act on what you hear. Small adjustments early on can make a big difference.

## Remember why you're doing this

Every church is one body with many parts (1 Corinthians 12:12–26). Digital changes should help everyone take part in church life, not just those who find technology easy.
