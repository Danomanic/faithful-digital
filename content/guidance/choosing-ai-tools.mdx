AI tools change quickly, so this page focuses on what to check rather than which products to use. Read [using AI well in your church](/guidance/using-ai-well/) first for the limits we recommend.

## Check how the tool uses your information

Before your church approves an AI tool, find out:

- whether what people type is used to train the provider's AI models, and whether you can turn this off
- how long the provider keeps what people type
- where the information is stored and processed
- whether the provider will sign data protection terms with your church
- who at the provider can see what people type

You'll usually find this in the provider's privacy policy, terms of use or business terms. If you can't find clear answers, don't use the tool for anything sensitive.

## Prefer accounts the church controls

Free personal accounts usually offer the least protection. Many AI tools offer business, team or charity plans with stronger privacy terms, for example a promise not to train on your information.

If your church uses Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, check what AI features are included or available in your plan. These may let people use AI within the same accounts and data protection terms the church already has.

Using church accounts also means you can remove someone's access when they step down.

## Match the tool to the task

| Task | What to use |
|---|---|
| Drafting a notice or summarising a public document | Most mainstream AI tools, with no personal information entered |
| Working with church documents that aren't public | A tool the church has approved, with suitable data protection terms |
| Anything involving personal, pastoral or safeguarding information | Don't use AI, unless your trustees have approved a specific tool for that purpose after careful checks |

## Check the age limits

Many AI tools have minimum ages, often 13 or 18. Don't encourage children or young people to use tools that aren't designed for them.

## Keep a list of approved tools

Write down which AI tools people can use for church work, and for what. Review the list at least once a year, because tools and their terms change.

If you're considering a tool that will make decisions about people, or process a lot of personal data, you may need to carry out a data protection impact assessment. The ICO's guidance on AI explains when.

## Agree your rules

Once you know which tools you'll allow, agree simple rules for using them. Read [agree your church's AI rules](/guidance/writing-an-ai-policy/).
