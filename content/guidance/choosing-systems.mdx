Most churches need tools for a few core jobs: email and documents, people and rotas, money and giving, and a website. You don't need the newest or most powerful tools. You need tools that your volunteers can use, that the church controls, and that you can afford next year as well as this year.

## Start with what you already have

Before you look at anything new, list the tools you use now and ask:

- what works well and what causes problems
- who uses it, and how confident they are
- what it costs each year, including anything paid personally by volunteers
- who controls the account

Keep what works. Change something only when it's causing a real problem, costing too much or putting data at risk.

## Decide what you need

Write down what you need a tool to do, in plain words. For example: "Send a rota to 40 volunteers each month, and let them swap shifts."

Sort your needs into:

- **must have:** the tool is no use without it
- **should have:** important, but you could work around it
- **nice to have:** useful, but not a deciding factor

This stops you choosing a tool because of impressive features you'll never use.

## Questions to ask about any tool

- Can our least confident volunteer use it?
- What will it cost in 3 years' time, including when we grow?
- Is there a charity discount, and do we qualify for it?
- Where is our data stored, and does the provider explain how it protects it?
- Can we get all our data out in a common format if we leave?
- Does it work with the other tools we use?
- What help is available, and when?

Because churches often hold [special category data](/guidance/data-protection/), check the provider's privacy and security information carefully before you sign up.

## Get the charity discounts you're entitled to

Many providers offer free or discounted plans for charities. Charity Digital Exchange, run by the charity Charity Digital, offers donated and discounted software to eligible UK charities.

Read the conditions carefully. Some offers are only for registered charities, and some only cover a limited number of users. If your church doesn't have its own charity number, read [getting charity discounts](/guidance/trustee-responsibilities/#getting-charity-discounts).

## Change one thing at a time

When you do change a system:

1. Agree the decision with your trustees.
2. Choose a quiet time of year, not Christmas or Easter.
3. Try the new system with a small group first.
4. Move only the data you still need, and check it arrived correctly.
5. Train people, and give extra help to anyone less confident.
6. Keep the old system available, read-only, for a short time.
7. Close the old account and delete data you no longer need.

Our [migration checklist](/templates/migration-checklist/) takes you through each step. Read [bring your trustees and congregation with you](/guidance/bringing-people-along/) for help with the people side of change.
