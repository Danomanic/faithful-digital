For many people, your website is the first place they meet your church. Its most important job is to help someone who has never been before to feel ready to come.

## What to put on it

Start with what a newcomer needs to know:

- when and where your services are, with a map and parking information
- what a service is like, and how long it lasts
- what you offer for children, and how they're kept safe
- whether your building is accessible, including step-free access and hearing loops
- how to get in touch, with a real person's name if possible
- a few recent photos of real people, used with their consent

Then add what your church family needs, such as events, how to give and how to join a group.

Keep it short. A small, accurate website is better than a large one that's out of date.

## Keep it in the church's hands

- register your domain name in the church's name, not a volunteer's
- give at least 2 trusted people access to the domain and website accounts
- set the domain to renew automatically, using a church payment method
- keep a record of who built the site and how to update it

If your domain name expires, your website and your email can both stop working.

## Choose how to build it

| Option | Good for | Things to consider |
|---|---|---|
| A website builder | Most small churches | Easy to update, with a monthly fee |
| A church website service | Churches wanting church-specific features | Often linked to a church management system |
| A custom site built by a volunteer or agency | Churches with specific needs | Can be hard to update when that person moves on |

Choose a tool that more than one person in your church can update. Read our [comparison of website tools](/compare/websites/).

## Make it accessible

Your website should work for everyone, including on a phone and with assistive technology. Read [make your digital work accessible](/guidance/accessibility/) for a checklist.

## Privacy and cookies

- publish your [privacy notice](/guidance/data-protection/) on your website
- if your site uses cookies that aren't strictly necessary, such as some analytics, check the ICO's guidance on when you need consent
- prefer privacy-friendly analytics that don't track people across other websites
- be careful about embedding content from other sites, as it may set its own cookies

## Keep it up to date

Ask one person to check the website every month. Update service times, remove past events and check that contact details still work. Put a reminder in their calendar.
