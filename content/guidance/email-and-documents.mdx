A shared set of tools for email, documents and calendars is the foundation of most churches' digital work. It's also where many churches have the biggest problems, with church business spread across volunteers' personal email accounts.

## Why church email matters

Use email addresses on your church's own domain name, such as office@yourchurch.org.uk. This means:

- people can trust that a message really comes from the church
- the address stays with the church when a volunteer moves on
- church information doesn't sit in personal accounts that the church can't access

You can create addresses for roles, such as treasurer@ or safeguarding@, so they pass from one person to the next.

## The two main options

Most churches choose either Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace. Both include email, a calendar, online file storage, documents, spreadsheets and video calls. Both offer free or discounted plans to eligible charities.

| Feature | Microsoft 365 | Google Workspace |
|---|---|---|
| Best for | Churches already using Word, Excel and Outlook | Churches that want a simple, browser-based setup |
| Desktop apps | Included in some paid plans, not in the free plan | Works mainly in a web browser |
| Free charity offer | Up to 300 licences for Microsoft 365 Business Basic, which includes web and mobile apps | Google Workspace for Nonprofits at £0, with up to 2,000 users |
| Paid charity discounts | Discounts on plans such as Business Premium | Discounts on Business Standard and Business Plus |

Offers last checked on 18 November 2026. Microsoft stopped offering free Business Premium licences to charities in July 2025. Check each provider's website for current offers and conditions.

## How to choose

Choose the one your volunteers already know best. Familiarity matters more than small differences in features.

Also consider:

- what tools your denomination or network uses, as sharing may be easier
- whether people need desktop apps, such as Word or Excel, on their own computers
- whether you already have a free plan from either provider through another route

## Check you're eligible

Both providers check that you're a charity before giving you the charity offer. Google says organisations must be charitable and in good standing, and that those operating under another organisation's registration may not qualify.

If your church doesn't have its own charity number, read [getting charity discounts](/guidance/trustee-responsibilities/#getting-charity-discounts) before you apply.

## Set it up well

- make at least 2 trusted people administrators
- create shared folders that belong to the church, not to individuals
- turn on 2-step verification or passkeys for everyone
- set up shared mailboxes for roles, so more than one person can respond
- agree who can share files outside the church

Read [set up accounts and logins](/guidance/accounts-and-logins/) for more on keeping accounts secure.
