Separate rules cover how you send emails and texts to people. They're in the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR), which sit alongside data protection law.

## Messages that are not marketing

You don't need special permission to send people messages they need, such as:

- rotas and reminders to volunteers about their role
- details of an event someone has booked
- replies to someone who has contacted you

These are sometimes called service messages. You still need a lawful reason to use the person's contact details. Keep these messages to the point, and don't add appeals or adverts for other events.

## Messages that are marketing

Under PECR, "marketing" includes more than selling. It covers messages that promote your church's aims, such as:

- newsletters inviting people to events
- appeals for donations or volunteers
- invitations to courses, such as an introduction to the Christian faith

To send these by email or text, you usually need the person's consent. Consent must be clear and freely given. A ticked box someone chose to tick, or a signed form, is fine. A pre-ticked box is not.

## The charitable purpose soft opt-in

Since 5 February 2026, charities can email or text marketing about their charitable purposes without consent, if they meet certain conditions. This is called the "charitable purpose soft opt-in".

You can only use it if:

- your church is a charity
- you got the person's details when they showed interest in or supported your work, for example by attending, giving or volunteering
- you gave them a clear, simple way to opt out when you collected their details
- you give them a way to opt out in every message

You can't use it for contact details collected before 5 February 2026. For those, you need consent, unless the messages are service messages.

<InsetText>

If you're not sure whether the soft opt-in applies, ask for consent. It's simpler, and people tend to welcome messages they asked for.

</InsetText>

## Good habits

- use a mailing tool, or send with all addresses in the "Bcc" field, so you don't share people's email addresses
- keep a record of when and how people agreed to hear from you
- act on every opt-out promptly, and don't contact that person for marketing again
- don't add people to a mailing list just because they gave you their details for something else

## Phone calls and group chats

Different rules apply to marketing phone calls. Check the ICO's guidance if your church makes calls to people who haven't asked to hear from you.

Adding someone to a group chat shares their phone number with everyone in the group. Ask before you add anyone. Read [news, social media and group messaging](/guidance/communications/) for more.
