Giving is an act of worship, and making it easy is a practical way to serve your congregation. Gift Aid can add a quarter to many gifts at no extra cost to the giver.

## Make giving easy

Fewer people carry cash, so offer more than one way to give. Common options are:

- **standing orders:** regular gifts made directly from the giver's bank, with no fees for the church
- **online giving:** a page or form on your website where people can give by card or bank transfer
- **card readers:** contactless giving at the back of church or at events
- **cash and cheques:** still important for some people

Check each provider's fees carefully. Small percentage fees add up over a year. Standing orders usually cost the church nothing.

Make sure people who don't use technology can still give easily.

## Claim Gift Aid

Gift Aid lets you claim back 25p from HMRC for every £1 donated by a UK taxpayer. To claim it:

- your church must be recognised by HMRC as a charity for tax purposes
- the giver must make a Gift Aid declaration, giving you permission to claim
- the giver must have paid at least as much Income Tax or Capital Gains Tax in that tax year as you claim

You usually claim online through HMRC's Charities Online service, or through software that connects to it.

Your denomination or network may have arrangements for claiming Gift Aid, so check with them first.

## The Gift Aid Small Donations Scheme

The Gift Aid Small Donations Scheme (GASDS) lets you claim a top-up payment on small cash and contactless donations of £30 or less, without a declaration. It's useful for collections at services.

The most you can claim on is the lower of:

- £8,000 of small donations in a tax year
- 10 times the amount of Gift Aid donations you claimed on in the same tax year

Extra rules apply to churches with more than one building, or that are linked to other charities. HMRC's guidance explains these.

Limits last checked on 18 November 2026.

## Keep good records

HMRC can ask to see records of your claims. Keep:

- every Gift Aid declaration, and a record of when it was made
- records that link each donation to its declaration
- records of cash collections, counted and signed by 2 people

Treat these records as personal data, and store them securely. Read [look after personal data](/guidance/data-protection/) for more.
