In many churches, digital work depends on one willing volunteer. When that person moves on or becomes unwell, things can quickly go wrong. Sharing the work makes it safer, and it's kinder to the people doing it.

## Roles to consider

You don't need a person for every role. In a small church, one person may hold several roles. What matters is that each role has a named person, and someone who can step in.

| Role | What they do |
|---|---|
| Lead trustee for digital | Makes sure the trustees understand and oversee digital work, and reports to them each year |
| Digital coordinator | Keeps the list of accounts and systems, and coordinates changes |
| Data protection lead | Keeps data protection records up to date, and responds to requests and breaches |
| Account administrators | At least 2 people who can manage each important account |
| Website and communications team | Keeps the website, newsletter and social media up to date |
| Safeguarding lead | Makes sure online activity follows your safeguarding policy |
| Helpers | People who can help others who are less confident with technology |

## Write it down

Keep a simple record of:

- who holds each role, and who covers for them
- what each person is responsible for
- where instructions are kept for regular tasks

Store this in a shared church folder, not in one person's account. Our [digital strategy one-pager](/templates/digital-strategy/) includes a simple table of roles.

## Plan for when people move on

When someone steps down from a role:

1. Agree a handover period, if possible.
2. Ask them to update the written instructions for their tasks.
3. Transfer any accounts or files they control to the church.
4. Remove their access to accounts they no longer need.
5. Thank them for their service.

Read [set up accounts and logins](/guidance/accounts-and-logins/) for more on removing access safely.

## Look after your volunteers

Digital work is often hidden. People notice when the website is out of date, but rarely when it's right.

- thank people publicly for their work
- give them a clear role, with realistic expectations
- offer training when they need it
- check that no one is carrying too much
- make it easy to step back without feeling guilty

## Getting outside help

Some tasks may need help from outside your church, such as setting up a new system. You could ask:

- your denomination or network, which may offer advice or shared services
- other local churches, which may have already solved the same problem
- skilled volunteers in your congregation, with a clear plan for handover

If you pay someone, make sure the church keeps control of all accounts, and that the work is written down.
