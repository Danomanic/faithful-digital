Your church's safeguarding policy should cover what happens online, as well as in person. The same principles apply: protect children and adults at risk, keep good boundaries, and know how to respond to concerns.

<InsetText>

Your church's safeguarding lead, and your denomination or network, should be your first point of contact. Follow their policies where they differ from this page.

</InsetText>

## Livestreams and recordings

If you stream or record services, Thirtyone:eight, an independent Christian safeguarding charity, recommends that:

- everyone in the room knows that the service is being streamed or recorded
- clear signs show where people can be seen on camera, and where to sit to stay out of view
- children's full names aren't mentioned during the service
- parents give written permission before their children appear on a stream or recording
- your church has an online safety policy that explains how people will be kept safe

Point cameras at the front, not at the congregation. Take extra care with prayer ministry, baptisms and testimonies, where people may share personal things.

Review older videos too, and remove any that put people at risk.

## Photos

- ask before taking photos, and say how you'll use them
- get written consent from a parent before using a photo of a child
- don't name children in photos you publish
- store photos in a church account, not on personal phones
- remove photos promptly if someone asks

## Online groups and meetings

For online groups and video meetings, especially with children or young people:

- use church accounts to host meetings, not personal ones
- have at least 2 approved adults present, as you would in person
- use waiting rooms or meeting passwords, so only invited people can join
- don't record meetings without telling everyone and having a reason to do so
- agree what you'll do if someone shares something worrying

## Messaging children and young people

Follow your church's code of conduct. Common good practice includes:

- getting consent from parents before contacting young people online
- using group messages rather than one-to-one messages
- including another approved adult in conversations
- keeping messages to church business, at sensible times of day
- not using personal social media accounts to contact young people

Check the minimum age for each app. Many messaging and social media apps have a minimum age of 13 or older.

## Responding to concerns

Online concerns should be handled in the same way as any other safeguarding concern. Make sure everyone involved in online activity knows:

- who your safeguarding lead is
- how to contact them
- not to delete messages or content that may be evidence, but to report them

If someone is in immediate danger, call 999.

## Where to get help

- your denomination's or network's safeguarding team
- Thirtyone:eight, which offers advice and a helpline
- NSPCC Learning, which has detailed guidance on online safety
