Your church's trustees are responsible for its digital work, even when volunteers or staff do it. This includes how the church looks after personal data, money and its online accounts.

Trustees don't need to be technical. They need to make sure the right things are in place, and that someone is doing them.

<InsetText>

In this blueprint, "trustees" means the people legally responsible for running your church. Depending on your church, they may be called a parochial church council (PCC), elders, deacons, a church council or a leadership team.

</InsetText>

## What trustees should make sure of

Your trustees should make sure that:

1. The church, not an individual, owns and controls its important accounts, domain name and data.
2. The church follows data protection law, and has a named person who leads on it.
3. Safeguarding policies cover online activity, such as livestreams and group messaging.
4. The church has reasonable protection against scams and cyber attacks.
5. Money spent on digital tools is good value, and contracts can be ended.
6. Any use of AI follows rules the trustees have agreed.
7. These arrangements are reviewed at least once a year.

You can record this in your trustee minutes. A short annual report from the person leading your digital work is usually enough.

## Make decisions together

Trustees should make important digital decisions together, not leave them to one enthusiastic person. Examples include choosing a church management system, changing email provider or agreeing an AI policy.

For each decision, the trustees should understand:

- what problem it solves
- what it costs now and in future years
- what personal data it involves
- what happens if the church wants to leave the service later

## How the rules differ across the UK

Charity law is different in each nation. Church denominations also have their own rules, so check with your denomination or network.

## Getting charity discounts

Many technology companies offer discounts to charities. They often check your status by asking for a charity registration number.

If your church is an excepted charity or part of a larger denomination, it may not have its own number. Ask your denomination or network, which may have arrangements in place. You can also contact the provider, explain how your church is governed, and ask what other evidence they accept. For example, this might be your HMRC charities reference number.

## Where to get help

- your denomination or network, which can often give advice on governance and policies
- the regulator for your nation
- a local council for voluntary service, which supports charities in your area
