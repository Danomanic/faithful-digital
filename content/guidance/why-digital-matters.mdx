Every church is entrusted with people, gifts and resources to care for. The tools we use are part of that trust.

It can be easy to think of websites, rotas and email lists as a distraction from the real work of ministry. Yet the people behind those systems are real: the family searching for a service time, the volunteer wondering when they're next on the rota, the housebound member hoping to hear Sunday's sermon.

When those small things work, love has fewer barriers to cross. A practical choice becomes an act of welcome.

## Practical work has always served ministry

The early church faced a problem of administration. In Acts 6, some widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution of food. The apostles didn't treat this as a lesser matter. The community chose people known to be full of the Spirit and wisdom to take care of it, and the word of God continued to spread.

We see in that story something that encourages every church administrator, treasurer and volunteer. Getting practical care right can be spiritual work, and it can free others to serve in their own calling.

The apostles also used the communication tools of their day. Paul wrote to the church at Colossae that he was with them in spirit even though he was absent in body (Colossians 2:5). His letters didn't replace his longing to be with God's people. They carried care across a distance until he could be there.

## Called to be faithful

Paul tells the Corinthians that those given a trust must prove faithful (1 Corinthians 4:2). We've taken our name from that idea.

Being faithful with digital tools doesn't require the latest technology or a big budget. It looks like:

- caring for people's personal information as carefully as we'd care for the people themselves
- making sure no one is shut out because of age, disability or confidence with technology
- spending the church's money wisely
- setting things up so that the work can continue when a volunteer steps down

These are everyday acts of stewardship, and they're within reach of any church.

## What digital can't do

We believe that the Word became flesh and lived among us (John 1:14). The gospel is deeply personal, and nothing replaces gathering together, sharing a meal, praying with someone or sitting beside them in grief.

Digital tools can invite people into that life and help sustain it. They can't be that life. No tool can guarantee growth or spiritual health, and we should be wary of anything that promises to.

So we can hold these tools with open hands: grateful for what they make possible, clear about their limits, and always asking how they might serve the people God has given us to love.

## Where to begin

You don't need to do everything at once. Start with prayer and a conversation with your team, then choose one small step.

Our [roadmap](/roadmap/) suggests a sensible order, and [setting up accounts and logins](/guidance/accounts-and-logins/) is a good first practical step for most churches.
