People in your church are probably already using AI tools. Agreeing some simple rules helps them do so safely, and shows your congregation that you've thought about it carefully.

## Decide how formal it needs to be

A small church may only need a conversation at a trustee meeting and a short note in the minutes. A larger church, with staff and many volunteers using AI, will usually benefit from a written policy.

Either way, make sure everyone who does church work knows what the rules are.

## What to include

A good AI policy for a church can usually fit on 1 or 2 pages. Include:

1. **Why you have the policy:** a sentence or two about your values, such as care for people and honesty.
2. **Approved tools:** which AI tools people can use for church work, and with which accounts.
3. **Good uses:** examples of tasks where AI can help.
4. **What stays human:** areas where you don't use AI, such as pastoral care and safeguarding decisions.
5. **Information that must not be entered:** for example personal details, pastoral conversations, safeguarding concerns, and health or financial information.
6. **Checking:** who checks AI-assisted work before it's used or published.
7. **Being open:** when you'll say that AI has been used.
8. **Questions and concerns:** who to ask, and how to report a mistake.
9. **Review:** when you'll review the policy, at least once a year.

Read [using AI well in your church](/guidance/using-ai-well/) for suggested limits and the reasons for them.

## Agree it together

1. Ask a trustee, or someone they appoint, to draft the policy.
2. Talk to staff and volunteers who use AI, to understand what they need.
3. Discuss the draft at a trustee meeting, including any questions faithful Christians may disagree about.
4. Agree the policy and record it in your minutes.
5. Share it with everyone who does church work, and explain the reasons for it.

## Keep it up to date

AI tools and laws are changing quickly. Set a date to review your policy, and review it sooner if you start using a new tool or something goes wrong.
